Slide 8 · What is an LLM

So, large language model. LLM for short. This is the kind of AI behind ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. And the name tells you what it is. Three words.

  • Large. It learned from a huge amount of writing. Trillions of words. Books, articles, and most of the public internet.
  • Language. What it learned from is human writing. That is why it reads and writes the way people do.
  • Model. It is a program that predicts. You ask it a question, and it writes one word. Then it guesses the most likely next word. Then the next. One word at a time, until it has a full answer.

Different companies make different models, and each one keeps trying to improve on the last. But the basic idea is the same.

Slide 9 · What AI is, and is not

So what does that mean for your business? Here is what it is, and what it is not.

It is predictive. It guesses the next word based on patterns it has seen.

It is a fast drafter. It is quick, it does not get tired, and it is confident. Remember that word, confident. We will come back to it.

And it reads and writes well. Newer tools also create documents, images, and spreadsheets.

Now the other side.

It is not magic. It does not understand things the way you do. It matches patterns. And because it is confident, it can be confidently wrong. It will give you a wrong answer in the same calm voice as a right one. You may hear this called a hallucination.

It does not know your business unless you tell it. It does not know your prices, your customers, or how you like to talk to them.

And most importantly, it is not a replacement for your judgment. You know what good looks like for your business. It does not.

If you remember one line from this section, make it this one: AI can draft, but you should decide.

Slide 10 · The vocabulary of AI

Thank you, Jennifer.

You are going to hear a lot of new words about AI. In the news, from vendors, from your nephew. Here are six worth knowing. You do not need to memorize them. If you remember what each one does, that is enough.

  • Prompt. A prompt is the message you type to the AI. It is your instruction. If you type, write a thank-you note to my landlord, that is a prompt. Daniel will show you how to write a good one later.
  • Context window. Think of this as the AI's short-term memory for one conversation. Everything you type, every file you add, and everything it writes back has to fit in that memory. In a very long conversation, it can start to forget what you said at the beginning. If that happens, start a new chat.
  • Tokens. Tokens are how the AI measures text. A token is a piece of a word. Roughly, one token is about one word. AI companies count tokens to set limits and prices. More text means more tokens, and more cost.
  • RAG. RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. That is a mouthful, so here is what it means. Retrieval: the AI looks something up. Augmented: it adds what it found. Generation: then it writes the answer. In plain words, you give the AI your own documents, and it answers from them instead of from the whole internet.

A free example is Google's NotebookLM. You upload your menu, your price list, or your store policies. Then you ask, what is our return policy? It answers from your document and shows you where it found the answer. Claude Projects and custom GPTs in ChatGPT work the same way. No coding needed.

  • Agentic AI. Agentic comes from the word agent. Most AI answers questions. An agent does tasks for you. It can open a website, click buttons, and fill out a form. For example, you could ask it to visit three supplier websites and compare prices. Because it acts on its own, this is where checking its work matters most.
  • Open source. Open source means the AI model is free for anyone to download and run. Llama from Meta, DeepSeek, Qwen, and Kimi K2 are examples. You can run them on your own computer, so your information stays with you. The tradeoff is setup. It takes some technical skill.

Slide 14 · Three checks

IRIS So now you have a PITA. How do you know if AI can help with it? We run it through three checks.

  • One. Procedure. Can you write down the steps? Think about reconciling your books. You go through your receipts, you put each one in a category, you add them up. Those are steps. That is a yes. Choosing a business partner? There is no list of steps for that. That is a no.

Slide 18 · Safety and privacy

Now we want to anchor this whole conversation in safety and privacy. Five rules.

  • One. Keep a human in the loop. AI is a great drafter. But you decide what goes out. Never let it send money, sign anything, hire anyone, or post on its own. You know what good looks like for your business. It does not.
  • Two. Keep private things out. No card numbers. No bank account numbers. No passwords. No Social Security numbers. No customer or employee records in a public AI tool.
  • Three. Assume free tools may learn from what you type. There is often a reason a tool is free. You can turn this off, and Yujia will show you how in a minute. Or use a paid business plan for sensitive work.
  • Four. AI makes things up. Jennifer said it is confident. When it does not know something, it can invent an answer instead of saying so. It can invent a business, a source, or a website that does not exist. So check names, numbers, and dates. For anything legal, tax, or medical, check with a person. And read anything that goes to customers like an editor.
  • Five. Start inside your business, not in front of customers. Your books, your inventory, your schedule. A chatbot that talks to customers and gets it wrong, gets it wrong in public. The back office is easier to check, and that is where you save the most time.

One more thing. Scammers use AI too. They can clone a voice from a short recording. Their phishing emails now read clean, with no spelling mistakes. If anyone asks for money or access in a way that feels unusual, even someone who sounds like your family or your bank, hang up and call them back on a number you already know.

So, rule three. Turning off training. Yujia is going to show you exactly where that setting is. Yujia.

Slide 26 · Using AI effectively

Thank you, Bryan.

Let's talk about the last piece, the prompt. A prompt is how you give AI direction. You saw it in Bhavna's demo.

Most owners use AI like Google. They type one line, get a weak answer, and then type no, that is not it, again and again. That back and forth is where people give up.

Look at the left side. Write me a caption. You get a caption that could belong to any business on any street.

Now the right side. Same job, four parts. Role and goal. You are my social media manager. Write an Instagram caption for my tea shop's new jasmine milk tea. Steps. Lead with the flavor, say it is new this week, end with a reason to visit. Output. Friendly, under 30 words, in English and Chinese. Eval. Check that it reads naturally in both languages.

Now you get a caption you can post. The quality of your input sets the quality of your output.

Slide 27 · The prompt framework

Here are the four parts.

  • Role and goal. Tell the AI who it is and what to get done.
  • Steps. Break the job into steps, the same way you did for the procedure check. Think of AI as a well-read intern. Hand an intern your books with no instructions and they will get it wrong. Whose fault is that?
  • Output. Say exactly what you want back. A table, an email, a post for Instagram. It cannot read your mind.
  • Eval. Ask it to check its own work. Confirm the totals add up. Confirm every line is counted once. This is the step most people skip, and it is the one that catches mistakes.

It takes a minute longer up front. It saves you the back and forth.
